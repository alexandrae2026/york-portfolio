Inventions

Study 25

A music box turns. Its cylinder carries Bach's Invention 4 in D minor as pins: the guitar's voice toward one end, the piano's toward the other. A pin sounds as it passes the comb along the top; the past turns down the front and the future comes over from behind.

A listener heard this piece with electrodes on the scalp, twice: once asked to follow the guitar, once the piano. Twenty of the notes tremble, ten in each voice; those pins are cut as waves, and the notes waver as they sound. The listener was asked to press a key whenever the voice being followed trembled. Each press is a bead above the cylinder, tied by a thread to the pin it answers: a clear bead when it answers the voice the listener was following, a smoky one when the listener's hand answered the other voice, and a dim bead alone beyond the guitar's end when it answers nothing. As each pin sounds, the listener's brain response to it is drawn as a comet from the pin, and heard as a faint echo after the note. The lit voice is the one the listener was asked to follow; each time the piece comes round, the hearing changes.

A second cylinder turns outside the listener's: a small transformer, trained on the listener's responses to four other inventions, guesses the response to each note before it comes, and from those guesses keeps a running belief about which voice the listener is following. Its pins are faint while it is right and brighten when it is surprised; its belief is the pale light that slides along the comb between the two voices.

Listener EBIP02, and the vibrato and key-press records, from Etard, Ben Messaoud, Gaugain and Reichenbach, Bipolar EEG dataset, music (Imperial College London, 2020; Zenodo 4470135, CC BY 4.0), described in their paper in the Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience 34 (2022). Model: a two-layer transformer trained on inventions 2, 3, 6 and 7 in both hearings, invention 4 held out. Designed by the artist; built and trained by Claude (Anthropic). © 2026 Alexandra Emberley.
